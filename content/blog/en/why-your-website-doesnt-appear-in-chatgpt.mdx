Two years ago, ranking on Google was enough.

With solid technical SEO, decent content, and a few backlinks, your business showed up when someone searched. Today, that logic no longer works on its own. More people ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini directly — and those systems decide which brands to mention without the user ever visiting your website.

The result: businesses with polished websites that are completely invisible in the new search layer.

## What GEO is and why it matters in 2026

**GEO** (Generative Engine Optimization) is the discipline of optimizing your digital presence so AI engines cite you, recommend you, and include you in their answers.

It doesn't replace traditional SEO — it complements it. Google remains critical. But if you only optimize for Google, you lose a growing share of searches that never generate a click.

In 2026, the relevant question isn't just "Do I rank on Google?" but **"Does AI mention me when someone asks about my sector?"**

## How ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini decide what to cite

LLMs don't crawl the web in real time like Google. They build answers from:

- **Indexed content** in their training data and live web access (when available)
- **Perceived authority** — mentions in sources the model considers reliable
- **Semantic clarity** — how easy it is to understand who you are, what you do, and where you operate
- **Consistency** — whether your brand is described the same way across multiple sources
- **Freshness** — recent content that reinforces your positioning

A business with a generic website, no schema markup, no external mentions, and no structured content has minimal chances of appearing in an AI response.

## 5 concrete things you can do today

### 1. Structure content as questions and answers

LLMs process content that answers direct questions more effectively. Instead of generic "About us" pages, create sections like:

- "What services does [your brand] offer in Mallorca?"
- "How much does a premium website cost for [your sector]?"
- "Why choose [your brand] over other agencies?"

Each answer should be clear, specific, and self-contained.

### 2. Add FAQ schema markup

The `FAQPage` schema helps both Google and AI systems identify question-answer pairs on your site. It's straightforward technical implementation — JSON-LD in the `<head>` of your key pages.

```json
{
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "What does Umania Labs do?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "We design premium websites with integrated AI..."
    }
  }]
}
```

### 3. Get mentioned in authoritative sources

LLMs weight what they find in media, industry directories, specialist publications, and verified profiles. This isn't link spam — it's **real presence** in ecosystems the model considers credible.

For a business in Mallorca: local directories, regional press, industry associations, collaborations with complementary brands.

### 4. Use consistent entity language

Define precisely: brand name, location, services, price range, target audience. Repeat that same formulation on your website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, directories, and any external mention.

Inconsistency confuses models. "Umania Labs, premium web design studio in Mallorca specializing in AI-powered websites for HNWI and startups" must be the same entity everywhere.

### 5. Publish regularly

LLMs favor fresh, cited content. A blog with monthly articles about your sector — real cases, data, methodology — reinforces your topical authority far more than a static 5-page website.

Volume isn't the point. **Consistency and depth** are.

> 97% of businesses don't appear in AI responses. Not because their product is bad — but because their digital presence isn't structured for this new channel.

## The next step

The good news: GEO is implementable in weeks, not years. It doesn't require rebuilding your website from scratch — it requires structure, schema, content, and consistency.
